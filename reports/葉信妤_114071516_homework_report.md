# Homework Report — Reddit Financial Sentiment Analysis

For this homework, I used the AI Agent and the data mining tools developed in AgentDev to analyze a new Reddit stock sentiment dataset. The dataset contains 847 posts, including 315 negative, 423 neutral, and 109 positive posts (`load_dataset_1`). I first checked the data quality and found no missing values, while 34 records were flagged in the duplicate check (`check_missing_2`, `check_duplicates_4`). Since the dataset was relatively small, I decided not to apply additional sampling and kept all 847 documents for the following analysis. After converting the text into a document-term matrix (DTM), I obtained 4,346 features with approximately 99.53% sparsity (`dtm_6`).

When I examined the term frequency and variance results, I noticed that many of the top words were common English words such as "the," "to," and "and" (`term_freq_7`, `variance_filter_9`). Although these words appeared frequently or had high variance, they did not tell me much about financial sentiment. I therefore wanted to see whether another filtering method could give more meaningful results. Using the same negative sentiment category and Top-K setting (k=100), I compared term frequency and TF-IDF filtering. The term frequency results included words such as "calls," "lot," and "actually" (`patterns_13`), while TF-IDF returned more finance- and policy-related terms, including "trump," "china," "market," and "tariffs" (`patterns_18`). In this case, TF-IDF helped me identify more relevant discussion topics, although this does not necessarily mean the words are better at distinguishing sentiment.

I also compared Pearson and Spearman correlations to check which words were more closely related to negative sentiment (`pearson_filter_10`, `spearman_filter_11`). Interestingly, both methods identified similar words, including "he," "his," "will," "trump," and "fuck." For example, "he" had a Pearson correlation of 0.1825 and a Spearman correlation of 0.2043, while "trump" had correlations of 0.1572 and 0.1614, respectively. Although these words ranked relatively high, the correlation values were still weak. I also noticed that "trump" appeared in the TF-IDF results (`patterns_18`), which suggests that different methods can identify some overlapping terms. However, I would not consider these words reliable sentiment indicators based on these results alone, since appearing frequently in negative posts does not necessarily mean that a word expresses negative sentiment.

After that, I applied the same TF-IDF filtering and Top-K setting to neutral and positive posts (`patterns_19`, `patterns_20`). I found that words such as "market," "just," and "like" appeared frequently across different sentiment categories. For instance, "market" had a support of 23 in negative posts and 37 in neutral posts, while "good" appeared in all three categories and had a higher relative support in positive posts. Most of the top 20 patterns were also single words rather than combinations of words. The neutral results included a pattern containing "stockbuyvest" and "com," which appears to be related to a website address. These results reminded me that frequent pattern mining can identify common terms, but the usefulness of those patterns still depends on the data quality and the question being studied. Also, because the category sizes differ, support counts alone are not enough to compare sentiment relevance.

Finally, I used PCA and UMAP to examine whether the three sentiment categories formed separate groups. The first two PCA components explained 31.99% of the total variance, but most posts from different categories overlapped (`reduction_14`). UMAP produced a different distribution, yet the sentiment categories were still mixed together (`reduction_15`). This does not prove that sentiment cannot be classified, since both figures only show two-dimensional representations of the data. Overall, I learned that the tools developed in AgentDev could be applied to a new dataset, but successfully running an analysis does not always mean the selected features can answer the original research question. For financial sentiment analysis, I think it is important to consider both the meaning of the words and their relationship with the sentiment labels.
